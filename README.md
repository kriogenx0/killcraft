# Killcraft

A real-time strategy game in the browser in the spirit of Command & Conquer. Harvest ore, cut wood, pump oil, keep your base powered, build from the sidebar, and destroy everything the enemy owns. Play the computer, or play other people online.

## Run it

1. `make setup` installs dependencies (Node 22+).
2. `make dev` starts the game server and the dev server, and opens the game at http://localhost:8000. Use `make dev PORT=9000` for another port (the game server uses `SERVER_PORT`, default 8081).

Other targets: `make test` (type-check, lint, and every test, including a live two-player match against the server), `make build` (production bundle in `dist/`), `make up` / `make down` (run the production image in Docker on `PORT`). `make help` lists everything.

## Balance simulations

`npm run sim -- --matches 200 --teams 4` plays computer-only matches headlessly (four players, one per corner, or `--teams 2`) across all CPU cores and prints a Markdown report: win rate by corner, match length, when each age was reached, what every unit and structure built, killed and traded, and what winners spent on compared with everyone else. Every match is seeded, so the same options give the same report. `--json out.json` saves the raw results, `--max 3600` caps a match in game seconds. The code is in `src/sim/`.

## Play online

Click **Play Online**, pick a name, then:

- **Quick match** pairs you with the next person who presses it.
- **Create game** lists your game in the lobby; anyone can click **Join**.

Each match runs on the server, which sends each player only what their side can see. If a player leaves or surrenders, the other wins.

To let people outside your network play, deploy the Docker image (`deploy/`) to any host that runs containers and keeps WebSockets open, such as Coolify, Fly.io or a VPS. It serves the game and the lobby on one port (8080 in the container), with `/health` and `/status` checks.

### Production: killcraft.kriogenx.com (Coolify)

The live game runs on Coolify from this repo's `main` branch:

- **Build pack:** Dockerfile, base directory `/`, Dockerfile location `/deploy/Dockerfile`
- **Port:** `8080` (exposed). No environment variables are required; the image sets `SERVER_PORT=8080` and `STATIC_DIR`.
- **Domain:** `https://killcraft.kriogenx.com` (Coolify's proxy issues the certificate and passes WebSockets on `/ws` through)
- **Health check:** `GET /health`
- **DNS:** an `A` record for `killcraft` on kriogenx.com pointing at the Coolify server's IP.

## How to play

- **Terrain:** maps roll over hills, and some have plateaus ringed by cliffs with ramps up, a river with fords, or a sea in one corner. Deep water, cliffs and woods block ground units. Shallow water (at the edges of lakes, rivers and the sea, and at fords) can be waded at half speed, and units pick their way round it when that's quicker. Aircraft ignore all of it. Each map also has its own time of day, from a cool morning to a golden evening.
- **Economy:** there are three resources, shown in the top bar.
  - **Nothing counts until it's carried home.** Every resource is gathered by a unit and only reaches your bank when that unit brings it back to base.
  - **Ore:** build a Power Plant, then a Refinery. Until the Modern age it comes with two **Workers** (peasants with a pick and a basket, trained at the Barracks; they heal like other infantry) who dig ore and carry it there; in the Modern age it comes with a Harvester instead. Each field holds about 14,000 ore and slowly regrows around its crystal rock; harvesters split a field between them instead of crowding one spot.
  - **Wood:** Workers chop it. Select Workers and right-click a tree (or a Lumber Mill, to chop in its woods): they walk to the tree, chop a load of 50 wood and carry it to the nearest Lumber Mill, Refinery or Town Hall, over and over. Wood only counts once it's dropped off, so build a Lumber Mill by the woods to shorten the trip. Each tree holds 150 wood, and a cut-down tree slowly grows back. Every side starts with woods near its base. Building comes first: when a site needs a Worker and none is free, one leaves the trees to build it.
  - **Oil:** from the start, an Oil Derrick (needs a Refinery) pumps oil from a well (the black pools: one near each base, two in the middle). Wells never refill, so a derrick runs dry; select it to see how much is left. Like a mill, a derrick fills its own tank (up to 300): right-click it with Workers selected and they carry the oil home.
  - **Salvage:** anything destroyed in battle leaves 30% of its ore price behind as ore on the ground (plus whatever a harvester was carrying), for either side's harvesters to collect.
  - **Aether (rare):** violet shards of a fallen star lie in two or three spots in the middle ground, glowing on the radar. Stand any unit beside a deposit, with no enemy unit there, and it picks up 2 Aether a second, up to 40 per unit. It counts once that unit walks back near your Town Hall or a Refinery (and is lost if it dies on the way). Hidden units appear the first time you bring some home; each deposit holds 200 and never regrows. Finding Aether reveals hidden units, each a regular unit plus one trick: the **Wraith Knight** (Medieval, lance passes through armor), the **Hex Rifleman** (World War, shots slow the target) and **Phantom Jeep** (World War, unseen until it fires or an enemy comes within 2 tiles), and the **Starfall Tank** (Modern, a beam that tears through buildings). The **Aether Ward** research makes your buildings take 10% less damage. Aether also builds supernatural structures: the **Star Shrine** (heals your units within 4 tiles, machines included), the **Ward Stone** (sees far and reveals cloaked enemies within 6 tiles), and from the World War age the **Waygate** (build two and your units step through one and out of the other; enemies can't use them).
  - Prices follow what a thing is made of and runs on: metal and stone cost ore, timber costs wood, and engines and rockets cost oil. A Knight's lance and a Crossbowman's bow take wood, a Catapult is mostly wood, and Jeeps, Tanks and Rocket Soldiers need oil. Aircraft too: a War Balloon is silk, wicker and a wood fire (ore and wood), a Biplane is a wood-and-canvas frame with an oil-burning engine (ore, wood and oil), and an Attack Helicopter is all metal and burns oil (ore and oil, no wood). Each cell in the sidebar shows its price.
- **Creatures of myth:** four lairs between the bases each hold a wild creature from Greek myth, picked per map from the **Centaur** (fast archer), **Minotaur** (axe brute that smashes buildings), **Cyclops** (hurls boulders at tanks and walls), **Hydra** (bites three foes at once and regrows its wounds), **Cerberus** (breathes fire over infantry), **Harpy**, **Pegasus** and **Griffin** (flyers). A wild creature attacks anything that comes close to its lair and can't be killed: beat it down to a quarter of its health and it kneels, then stand beside it with no enemy there for 5 seconds to capture it. Once a side has captured a kind, it can train more of them (flyers at the Airfield, the rest at the Barracks) for ore plus Aether. A kneeling creature nobody captures gets back up after 30 seconds. The computer sends hunting parties to capture them too.
- **Power:** every structure but the Power Plant uses power. If you use more than you make, production runs at half speed and turrets fire slowly.
- **Building:** click a structure in the sidebar (or select the Town Hall with H and press its letter), then click the map within 3 tiles of your base to place it. Hold Shift to place several in a row. Walls are dragged out: press where the line starts and release where it ends, and every segment becomes its own site, paid for as it goes down. The whole price is paid when you place it; if you can't afford all of it, it can't be placed. **Workers build it:** placing a site sends your nearest free Worker, and a site only goes up while Workers work on it. One Worker builds a structure in its build time, two in half that (half speed on low power). Select more Workers and right-click a site to add hands. A thin bar over the site shows how far along it is, any number of sites can go up at once, and a site starts weak and gains health as it rises, so guard it. Select a site and press Cancel or `Delete` (or right-click its sidebar picture) to call it off and get the whole price back. Units and research still queue: right-click one in the sidebar to put it on hold, again to cancel (or use its × button).
- **Ages:** there are three: Medieval, World War and Modern. Oil is the way forward. Every side starts Medieval; once it has an Oil Derrick on a well it can build the **Engine Works**, and finishing that takes it into the World War age. The Modern age is then researched at the Town Hall (it appears at the end of the structure list) and costs oil alone. Older units stay available after you advance, and the top bar shows your current age. Your base starts primitive in the Medieval age: a **Town Hall** that is a timber longhouse inside a log palisade, a post mill, clay furnaces, a thatched roundhouse. Upgraded, the Town Hall becomes a brick civic hall with a columned porch and a clock tower (World War), then a glass and concrete tower with a rooftop helipad (Modern). Reaching an age doesn't restyle it: each building keeps the look of the age it was built in until you **upgrade** it (select it and press Upgrade, or U). An upgrade costs half the building's ore and wood price, takes a little over half its build time (half speed on low power), and rebuilds it in brick and iron for the World War age or concrete and steel for the Modern age. Each age a building is upgraded through gives it 40% more health and 40% more of what it does: power, production speed, oil output, and damage for defenses. A thin gold bar over the building shows the progress, the same as a building going up; pressing Upgrade again cancels it for a full refund. New buildings go up in your current age's style. Walls are a different structure each age, so they don't upgrade. The sidebar pictures show your current age.

  | Age | How you get there | Unlocks |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Medieval | (start) | Swordsman, Crossbowman, Knight, Catapult, Harvester, the Watchtower, Lumber Mill, Oil Derrick and Engine Works, and every base structure but the Gun Turret |
  | World War | Build the Engine Works (1000 ore, 150 wood, 35s; needs an Oil Derrick) | Rifleman, Machine Gunner, Scout Jeep, Battle Tank, Gun Turret, Flak Cannon, Airfield, War Balloon, Biplane |
  | Modern | Research at the Town Hall: 800 oil, 60s | Rocket Soldier, Heavy Tank, Attack Helicopter, Combat Drone |

- **Bounty on the leader:** a side whose army and buildings are worth 30% more than anyone else's is in the lead. Everyone is told, and computer players turn on it. A unit that destroys one of its units or buildings picks up half that price in ore as loot, which counts once the unit brings it near its own Town Hall or a Refinery. If the carrier dies, the loot is left on the ground as ore.
- **Defense:** the Watchtower (Medieval, needs Barracks) shoots ballista bolts that are strong against infantry and cavalry; the Gun Turret (World War) fires shells that beat vehicles; the Flak Cannon (World War) only shoots at aircraft, from long range.
- **Units:** click a unit in the sidebar to queue it at your Barracks or War Factory. You start with your Town Hall and two Workers, and no army. The Town Hall trains Workers until you have a Barracks.
- **Workers mend things:** a Worker with nothing to carry looks after what's near it. It builds sites, repairs damaged buildings (paying ore at the same rate as the Repair button), and patches up wounded troops and vehicles at half a Medic's pace. Select Workers and right-click one of your damaged buildings, building sites or wounded units to send them to it. A Worker swings its pick at an enemy that attacks it or comes right up to it, but it's no soldier. On the passive stance it runs from enemies instead, and mends only when none are about. The computer sends Workers to its sites and badly damaged buildings too.
  - Barracks: swords and bows first, then guns, then rockets: Swordsman (plate armor, beats riflemen up close), Crossbowman (armor-piercing bolts), Knight (fast armored lancer), then Rifleman, Machine Gunner (belt-fed gun, mows down infantry but moves slowly) and Rocket Soldier.
  - War Factory: Catapult (lobs boulders from long range, crushes buildings) and Harvester, then Scout Jeep, Battle Tank and Heavy Tank.
  - Airfield (World War, needs a Refinery): War Balloon (slow, sees far, drops bombs), then Biplane (fast fighter, best against other aircraft) and, in the Modern age, the Attack Helicopter (missiles that wreck tanks) and the Combat Drone (a cheap, fast quadcopter with a light gun, best in swarms). Aircraft fly straight over buildings, woods and water.
  - Only weapons that can aim up hit aircraft: crossbows, ballista towers, rifles, machine guns, rockets, aircraft guns and flak. Swords, lances, catapults and tank cannons can't. Rocket Soldiers and the Flak Cannon are the best answers to helicopters.
  - Rockets beat armor, rifles beat unarmored infantry, swords beat rifles, crossbows pierce plate, tanks beat almost everything.
- **Orders:** left-click or drag to select. Right-click to move, send harvesters to ore or a refinery, or set a factory's rally point. Units that can fight can **Patrol** (`T`, then click a point): they walk back and forth between where they are and that point, fighting by their stance on the way. Select a building to repair or scrap it (scrapping leaves part of its price on the ground as ore for your Workers to carry home).
- **Stances:** there is no separate attack order. Units only move, and each unit's stance decides how it fights. Pick one from the command card for a unit or a whole selection:
  - Units that fight always turn on whoever attacks them first, and never keep hitting a building or harvester that can't fight back while an armed enemy is within reach.
  - **Stop & attack** (default): the unit stops to fight enemies it meets, then carries on to where it was going. Idle units engage anything in sight.
  - **Move & attack** (attack while moving): the unit shoots whatever is in range without stopping or turning aside. Idle, it holds its ground and fires.
  - **Don't attack:** the unit holds fire and runs away from armed enemies that come close or shoot at it.
- **Touch:** tap to select; with something selected, tap elsewhere to give an order. Drag to scroll.
- **Surrender** (online): the button in the top bar, clicked twice.
- **Camera (3D):** `W` `A` `S` `D`, the arrow keys or the screen edge to pan; `Q`/`E` or middle-drag to turn; scroll (mouse wheel or trackpad) to pan, `Shift`+wheel to pan sideways; `Ctrl`/`Cmd`+wheel or trackpad pinch to zoom; `Alt`+wheel or middle-drag up and down to tilt. On touch screens: drag with one or two fingers to pan, pinch to zoom, twist with two fingers to turn.
- **Keys:** `M` move, `T` patrol, `X` stop, `G` stop & attack, `F` move & attack (attack while moving), `V` don't attack, `R` repair, `U` upgrade a building to your age, `Delete` (or `Backspace`) scrap, `Ctrl`+`1-9` assign a group and `1-9` recall it, `H` select the Town Hall and jump to it, `Space` jump to the last alert, `+`/`-` game speed, `P` pause.
- **Production hotkeys:** every structure, unit and research has a letter, shown on its sidebar button and lit while the building that makes it is selected. Select the Town Hall (`H`) and press a structure's or research's letter; select a Barracks, War Factory or Airfield (a control group works well) and press a unit's letter. Press a finished structure's letter again to place it. The Town Hall has more structures than free letters, so walls (`L`), defenses (`G`) and Aether structures (`T`) sit in groups: press the group key, then the item's letter (the sidebar shows both, e.g. `L T` for a Stone Wall; `Esc` backs out). Any building with too many items can do the same with `group` in its definition. Letters come from each item's name (`views/game/hotkeys.ts`), never use a camera key, and a definition can pick its own with `key`, so new units and buildings get one automatically.
